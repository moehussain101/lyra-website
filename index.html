It can see your screen. So it's built to mind its own business.

An app that watches your screen and hears your voice has to earn that. Here's how Lyra handles it.

Mic off until you wake it

Lyra isn't listening in the background. The mic only turns on while you're holding Shift + Option to talk — let go, and it's off again.

Your screen stays on your Mac

Lyra looks at your screen to find the button or file a task needs — that looking happens locally, on your machine, while the task runs.

Nothing recorded unless you say so

No screen recordings quietly piling up, no voice clips kept. Lyra doesn't record or save what it sees and hears unless you tell it to.

Every shortcut, in one place.

No manual, no settings dive. This is the whole cheat sheet.

⇧ Shift+⌥ Option Speak to Lyra

Hold both keys anywhere and talk. Works while Lyra is riding under your cursor. Let go when you're done — the mic turns off.

⌥ Option×2 Type to Lyra

Double-press Option to open a typing bubble next to your cursor instead of speaking — for quiet rooms and long tasks.

Hover Open the notch

Move your cursor to the notch pill at the top of the screen and it expands — music, clipboard, file shelf, calendar, and Lyra.

✶ Menu bar Open Lyra from the top menu

Tap Lyra's little button in the menu bar, from any app, to open the notch and give it a task manually.

A · S · A · D

The ASAD sequence.

Every task Lyra does runs the same four-step loop: Ask, See, Act, Done. It's the whole idea behind Lyra in four words — you ask in plain language, Lyra sees your screen, acts on your Mac itself, and finishes the job. Here's each step, properly.

Ask→ See→ Act→ Done
Step 1 · A — Ask

You ask, in your own words

No commands to memorize, no menus to dig through. You give Lyra a task the way you'd say it out loud — by voice, holding Shift + Option, or by typing after a double-press of Option. If the task is fuzzy, this is where "done" gets defined: what you want, in your words.

You: "Open my science slides and make a practice quiz."
Step 2 · S — See

Lyra sees your screen

Before touching anything, Lyra looks. It sees what's open, what's on screen, and where the exact button, field or file for the task is — the same way a person sitting next to you would look before pointing. Seeing first is why it clicks the right thing instead of guessing.

Lyra: "Your slides are open. The quiz topics are on slides 12–30."
Step 3 · A — Act

Lyra does it — not just explains it

This is the step that makes Lyra different from a chatbot. It doesn't hand you instructions; it drives the Mac itself — clicking, typing, opening apps, moving files — step by step, while you watch it work and can see exactly what it's doing.

Lyra: Pulling the key topics, writing 10 questions, saving the quiz…
Step 4 · D — Done

Done means done

The loop only ends when the task is actually finished — the quiz exists, the downloads are sorted, the schedule is on your calendar. Then Lyra says so, folds back into the notch, and gets out of your way: weather on the left, battery on the right.

Lyra: "Done. 10-question practice quiz, ready when you are."

One loop, every task.

Quiz from slides, a sorted Downloads folder, a study schedule — different tasks, same ASAD sequence underneath. Ask, See, Act, Done. If a task needs another pass, the loop simply runs again — and you stay in control the whole time, watching every step.
